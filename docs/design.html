Principles

One phosphor

Everything that is only text comes from a single amber at four brightness levels. Hierarchy is brightness, the way it was on a monochrome tube.

Honest signals

Red means removed or failed, green means added or passed, yellow warns. These are never tinted amber. An all-amber scheme looks right and makes a diff unreadable.

Read for hours

Every effect is set for a working day in front of it. Scanlines at a third of the web strength, no flicker, and no colour used for text below a contrast it can carry.

Physics, not decoration

The glow comes from the lit pixels themselves and spreads to their neighbours. Nothing is painted on top that a tube would not have produced.

Roles before colours

Each target maps from a role such as textDim or error, not from a hex value. Change the phosphor and every terminal follows.

One source

A single token file produces the files for Windows Terminal, Claude Code, Ghostty, kitty, Alacritty, CSS and the shader constants.

Where it comes from

The amber monitor

Monochrome monitors of the 1980s drew text with one phosphor. Amber was sold as the easier colour to read for long sessions. The theme takes its amber, #FFB000, from eiDotter, whose token describes it as P3 phosphor at 602nm.

The CGA palette

IBM's Color Graphics Adapter of 1981 offered sixteen colours from four bits: red, green, blue and intensity. The signal colours here are those values. The dark red and dark blue are skipped for text, for the reason in the palette section.

eiDotter's rules

Two lines from the design system's principles set the bar: "Phosphor as physics, not decoration", and "The aesthetic is a constraint the theme holds itself to, not decoration".

The scheme it replaces

The earlier terminal scheme mapped all sixteen slots to shades of amber and orange. It was pure, and it erased the difference between an added and a removed line. That failure is the reason for the second principle.

The phosphor

Seven values from black to white. The number under each is its contrast against the screen colour; 4.5 is the WCAG AA line for body text.

black#050300 · screen
tint#1A1000 · 1.10
amberDim#9A5700 · 3.67
amberSoft#B87C1A · 5.82
amber#FFB000 · 11.24
amberBright#FDCA9F · 13.86
white#FFFCF7 · 20.13
  • black is a warm near-black, because an unlit tube is not pure black.
  • amberDim is for borders and selection only. At 3.67 it is too dark for running text.
  • amberSoft is the dimmest amber that passes AA, so it takes every "secondary text" job.

  • Yellow is lemon, not gold, so a warning stands apart from amber body text.
  • Two dark fills sit behind diff lines: #122010 for added and #430500 for removed, both from eiDotter's alert surfaces.

The monitor

A pixel shader draws the tube over whatever the terminal renders. Its knobs carry the names of eiDotter's effect tokens, with lower values.

TokenHereeiDotterWhat it does
scanline.intensity0.200.6How dark the gap between raster rows gets.

phosphor.glowIntensity0.35noneStrength of the tight glow.
phosphor.bloomRadius4.5 px80 pxSoft halation further out.
phosphor.bloomIntensity0.220.5Strength of the halation.

vignette.spread0.200.20How far in the dimming reaches.
flicker.intensity00.04Off.

The radii differ in kind, not only in size. eiDotter's 40 and 80 pixels are an inset glow around the edge of a web page. Here the glow is sampled from the picture, so each glyph lights its own surroundings.

Left out

  • Phosphor persistence. The trail a tube leaves when text changes needs the previous frame, and a Windows Terminal shader only sees the current one.
  • Flicker. Left out on purpose.
  • Curvature. Bending the picture would move it away from where mouse clicks land.

What could flow back

  • The aperture grille and per-pixel glow, as a canvas or WebGL layer for the web.
  • A "long reading" preset of the effect tokens at these lower values.
  • The role layer for terminals as an official eiDotter target.

What stays different

  • Honest red and green by default. On the web that remains a choice of theme.
  • No display typeface.
  • No motion of any kind.
